\title{\textbf{CRAFT\,V3, V4, V5 and V8:\\ what each one changes relative to plain CRAFT}}
\author{Notes tied to the code in \code{models/CRAFT*.py}}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This note explains, step by step and with pointers to the exact code, how
\code{CRAFTV3}, \code{CRAFTV4} and \code{CRAFTV5} differ from the base
\code{CRAFT} model for future--link prediction. All three keep CRAFT's overall
recipe: embed the recent neighbours, cross--attend, glue on the time features,
score with a small MLP, train with BPR/BCE. The \emph{only} thing that changes
is how the ``edge representation'' vector is built. V3 rewrites the attention
formula, V4 adds one summary vector and mixes it in through a gate, and V5 adds
a second attention that runs in the opposite direction and blends the two.
V8 leaves CRAFT's edge representation untouched and instead adds a correction to
the final score, read from evidence outside CRAFT's history window: the
direction of past interactions, the whole past of the pair, and other users'
co--visits.
Measured results on several datasets are at the end.
\end{abstract}

\section{Symbols used everywhere}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item $B$ --- batch size (number of source events in one step).
  \item $L=\code{num\_neighbors}$ --- how many recent neighbours we keep per node.
  \item $D=\code{embedding\_size}$ --- size of every node vector.
  \item $h=\code{num\_heads}$, and $d_h=D/h$ --- per--head size.
  \item $N=1+n_{\text{neg}}$ --- candidates scored per source. Candidate $0$ is
        the true next destination; the rest are negatives.
  \item $\bm{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{B\times L}$ --- \code{src\_neighb\_seq}: the source's
        $L$ most recent neighbours (these are \emph{destination--role} nodes).
  \item $\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{B\times N}$ --- \code{test\_dst}: the candidate
        destination ids.
  \item $\Emb(\cdot)$ --- the node embedding table \code{node\_embedding};
        $\pos$ --- \code{position\_embedding}; $\LN$ --- \code{LayerNorm};
        $\sigma$ --- the logistic sigmoid; $\elt$ --- element--wise product.
\end{itemize}

\section{Base CRAFT, one step at a time}
\label{sec:craft}

Code: \code{models/CRAFT.py}, method \code{forward}.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.},leftmargin=3.2em]
  \item \textbf{Embed the source's history} (the ``look--up table'' the
        candidates will read from).
        \[
          \bm{H} \;=\; \LN\big(\Emb(\bm{s}) + \pos\big)\ \in\ \R^{B\times L\times D}.
        \]
        One row of $\bm{H}$ is one past neighbour, with a position marker added.

  \item \textbf{Embed the candidates} (the ``questions'' we want answered).
        \[
          \bm{Q} \;=\; \LN\big(\Emb(\bm{d})\big)\ \in\ \R^{B\times N\times D}.
        \]

  \item \textbf{Cross--attention: every candidate reads the history.}
        Inside one head, with $Q\in\R^{N\times d_h}$ and $K,V\in\R^{L\times d_h}$:
        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
          \item raw match scores: \(\displaystyle S = \frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_h}}\)\quad(shape $N\times L$);
          \item hide padded history slots: \(S \leftarrow S + M\) (with $M_{ij}=-\infty$ where slot $j$ is padding);
          \item turn scores into weights: \(A = \softmax(S)\) row--wise (each candidate's weights over the $L$ slots sum to $1$);
          \item read the values: \(\text{context} = A\,V\)\quad(shape $N\times d_h$).
        \end{enumerate}
        Then the usual wrap--up: linear projection, dropout, $\LN$, add the
        query back (residual), and a feed--forward block. Call the result
        \[
          \bm{O}\ \in\ \R^{B\times N\times D}.
        \]
        $\bm{O}$ is the \emph{edge representation}: one vector per
        (source, candidate) pair.

  \item \textbf{Attach the time features.} Compute the gap since each candidate
        was last seen, \(\bm{\tau}=\phi_t(\code{cur\_times}-\code{dst\_last\_update\_times})\),
        and (optionally) a ``how often has this candidate appeared in the
        history'' count. Concatenate:
        \(\bm{O} \concat \bm{\tau} \concat \cdots\).

  \item \textbf{Score.} \(\hat{y} = \mathrm{MLP}_{\text{out}}(\bm{O}\concat\bm{\tau}\concat\cdots)\in\R^{B\times N\times 1}\).

  \item \textbf{Loss.} BPR (or BCE), treating candidate $0$ as positive and the
        rest as negatives.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What V3/V4/V5 never touch.} Steps \textbf{C1, C2, C4, C5, C6} and the
embedding table are identical in all variants. Every change is inside
step \textbf{C3} --- the box that produces $\bm{O}$.

\section{CRAFT\,V3 --- attend twice, sharpen in between}
\label{sec:v3}

Code: \code{CustomMultiHeadCrossAttention.forward} in \code{models/CRAFTV3.py}.
The rest of the model is copied from CRAFT unchanged.

\paragraph{The one--sentence idea.}
Run attention once just to see which past neighbours matter \emph{on average},
shrink the unimportant ones, then run attention again for real.

\paragraph{Step by step (one head).}
Start from $Q\in\R^{N\times d_h}$, $K,V\in\R^{L\times d_h}$, mask $M$.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{V3.\arabic*},leftmargin=3.6em]
  \item \textbf{First attention weights.}
        \[
          A_1 \;=\; \softmax\!\Big(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_h}} + M\Big)\ \in\ \R^{N\times L}.
        \]
        Row $i$ = how candidate $i$ would spread its attention over the $L$ slots.

  \item \textbf{Average down to one profile.} Collapse the candidate axis by
        taking the mean of each column:
        \[
          s_j \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(A_1)_{ij},
          \qquad \bm{s}=(s_1,\dots,s_L)\ \in\ \R^{L}.
        \]
        $s_j$ = ``how much did history slot $j$ matter, averaged over all
        candidates.'' This single profile is shared by everyone in the row.

  \item \textbf{Normalise (a safety step).}
        \[
          \hat{s}_j \;=\; \frac{s_j}{\sum_{k=1}^{L} s_k + \varepsilon}.
        \]
        Each $A_1$ row already sums to $1$, so $\sum_k s_k$ is already $\approx 1$;
        the $\varepsilon$ just avoids dividing by zero.

  \item \textbf{Shrink the keys of unimportant slots.} Scale every key vector by
        its profile weight:
        \[
          K'_j \;=\; \hat{s}_j\, K_j \qquad (j=1,\dots,L).
        \]
        A slot with a small $\hat{s}_j$ now has a tiny key, so no candidate can
        attend to it much.

  \item \textbf{Second attention, for real.}
        \[
          A_2 \;=\; \softmax\!\Big(\frac{QK'^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_h}} + M\Big),
          \qquad
          \text{context} \;=\; \mathrm{dropout}(A_2)\,V.
        \]

  \item \textbf{Wrap--up} (projection, dropout, $\LN$, residual, feed--forward):
        exactly as in CRAFT step \textbf{C3}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{A shortcut view.} Because $K'_j=\hat{s}_j K_j$, step V3.5 is the same
as taking the first--pass raw scores and multiplying column $j$ by $\hat{s}_j$
before the softmax:
\[
  (\text{score}_2)_{ij} \;=\; \hat{s}_j\cdot\frac{q_i\cdot k_j}{\sqrt{d_h}} + M_{ij}.
\]
So a slot that got little average attention is pushed toward zero for
\emph{every} candidate.

\paragraph{In plain words.}
Plain CRAFT lets each candidate look at the history however it likes. V3 first
holds a vote --- ``which of my last $L$ interactions are generally useful right
now?'' --- and then makes all candidates look through that same sharpened lens.
Good when a few past items clearly dominate; bad when the history is genuinely
varied, because the shared lens throws away detail that some candidates needed.

\section{CRAFT\,V4 --- add a ``recent intent'' vector through a gate}
\label{sec:v4}

Code: \code{models/CRAFTV4.py}. The original \code{CrossAttention} is reused
as--is; new parts are \code{behavior\_attn} / \code{behavior\_proj} (build the
vector) and \code{proj\_c} / \code{proj\_z} / \code{gate\_layer} (mix it in).
Mode chosen by \code{--fusion\_mode} (\code{projected} default).

\paragraph{The one--sentence idea.}
Keep CRAFT's answer, then gently correct it using one vector that summarises
what the source has been doing lately.

\paragraph{Part A --- CRAFT's answer, unchanged.}
\[
  \bm{c}_d \;=\; \CA(\bm{Q},\ \bm{H})\ \in\ \R^{B\times N\times D}
  \qquad\text{(CRAFT step \textbf{C3}).}
\]

\paragraph{Part B --- build the ``recent intent'' vector $\bm{z}_t$.}
This pools the \emph{same} history $\bm{H}$ into a single vector per source
(\code{extract\_source\_behavioral\_state}).

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{V4.\arabic*},leftmargin=3.6em]
  \item \textbf{Score each history slot} with a tiny 2--layer network:
        \[
          e_\ell \;=\; \bm{w}_2^{\top}\tanh\!\big(W_1\,\bm{H}_\ell\big)\ \in\ \R
          \qquad (\ell=1,\dots,L).
        \]

  \item \textbf{Mask padding and normalise} into weights that sum to $1$:
        \[
          \alpha_\ell \;=\; \softmax_\ell(e_\ell),
          \qquad \alpha_\ell = 0 \text{ if slot }\ell\text{ is padding}.
        \]

  \item \textbf{Weighted sum} of the history:
        \[
          \bm{p} \;=\; \sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\alpha_\ell\,\bm{H}_\ell\ \in\ \R^{B\times D}.
        \]

  \item \textbf{Project} to get the final vector:
        \[
          \bm{z}_t \;=\; \mathrm{Dropout}\big(\LN(W_z\,\bm{p})\big)\ \in\ \R^{B\times D}.
        \]
        One vector per source, the same for all $N$ candidates.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Part C --- gated mix (\code{projected} mode).}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{V4.\arabic*},leftmargin=3.6em,start=5]
  \item \textbf{Put both on the same footing.}
        \[
          \tilde{\bm{c}} = \mathrm{proj}_c(\bm{c}_d),
          \qquad
          \tilde{\bm{z}} = \mathrm{proj}_z(\bm{z}_t)\ \text{(copied to all $N$ candidates)},
          \qquad \tilde{\bm{c}},\tilde{\bm{z}}\in\R^{B\times N\times D}.
        \]

  \item \textbf{Compute the gate} --- one number in $(0,1)$ per feature channel:
        \[
          \bm{g}_d \;=\; \sigma\!\big(W_g\,[\,\tilde{\bm{c}}\concat\tilde{\bm{z}}\,]\big)\ \in\ (0,1)^{B\times N\times D}.
        \]

  \item \textbf{Combine} --- CRAFT's vector plus a gated dose of intent:
        \[
          \bm{h}_d \;=\; \tilde{\bm{c}} \;+\; \bm{g}_d \elt \tilde{\bm{z}}.
        \]
\end{enumerate}
$\bm{h}_d$ then takes the place of $\bm{O}$; steps \textbf{C4--C6} are unchanged.
(\code{simple} mode does the same with no projections:
$\bm{h}_d=\bm{c}_d+\sigma(W_g[\bm{c}_d\concat\bm{z}_t])\elt\bm{z}_t$.)

\paragraph{In plain words.}
$\bm{c}_d$ answers ``does this candidate fit the source's history?''. $\bm{z}_t$
is a one--vector fingerprint of ``what the source is into lately,'' identical for
all candidates. The gate decides, channel by channel, how much fingerprint to
add: $0$ means ignore it (and because of the $+\tilde{\bm{c}}$ term we are back
to plain CRAFT), $1$ means add it fully. The extra term is only ever
\emph{added} on top of CRAFT, so V4 cannot damage CRAFT's own answer --- which
is why it is the safest variant.

\section{CRAFT\,V5 --- also attend the other way, then blend}
\label{sec:v5}

Code: \code{models/CRAFTV5.py}, plus small plumbing:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \code{utils/utils.py}: \code{get\_dst\_neighbors} fetches $L$ recent
        neighbours \emph{for every candidate}, using the \emph{same sampler and
        strategy} as the source side, and also returns the ``last seen'' time
        (other models still ask for just one neighbour, so nothing changes for
        them).
  \item \code{train\_link\_prediction.py} / \code{evaluate\_models\_utils.py}:
        the extra tensors \code{src\_node\_ids}, \code{dst\_neighb\_seq},
        \code{dst\_neighb\_interact\_times} are passed only for V5.
  \item New table \code{src\_node\_embedding} (source--role ids), new attention
        stack \code{rev\_cross\_attention}, new \code{gate\_layer}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The one--sentence idea.}
Plain CRAFT is one--directional: only the destination looks at the source's
history. V5 also runs the mirror image --- the source looks at each candidate's
history --- and blends the two answers with a gate.

\paragraph{View 1 --- forward (this is just CRAFT).}
\[
  \bm{h}_{\text{fwd}} \;=\; \CA(\bm{Q},\ \bm{H})\ \in\ \R^{B\times N\times D}.
\]
``Does candidate $d$ match what source $u$ recently interacted with?''

\paragraph{View 2 --- backward (new).}
Let $\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{B\times N\times L}$ be each candidate's own recent
neighbours and $\bm{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{B}$ the source ids.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{V5.\arabic*},leftmargin=3.6em]
  \item \textbf{Embed each candidate's history} with the source--role table
        $\Emb_{\mathrm{src}}$ (helper \code{encode\_neighbor\_seq}, same recipe as
        CRAFT step \textbf{C1}):
        \[
          \bm{G} \;=\; \LN\big(\Emb_{\mathrm{src}}(\bm{r}) + \pos\big)
          \ \in\ \R^{(B\cdot N)\times L\times D}.
        \]

  \item \textbf{Embed the source node itself} and copy it to every candidate:
        \[
          \bm{q}_{\text{src}} \;=\; \LN\big(\Emb_{\mathrm{src}}(\bm{u})\big)\ \in\ \R^{B\times 1\times D}
          \ \longrightarrow\ \R^{(B\cdot N)\times 1\times D}.
        \]

  \item \textbf{Cross--attend the other way} (query $=$ the source,
        keys/values $=$ the candidate's history):
        \[
          \bm{h}_{\text{bwd}} \;=\; \CA_{\text{rev}}(\bm{q}_{\text{src}},\ \bm{G})
          \ \longrightarrow\ \R^{B\times N\times D}.
        \]
        ``Does source $u$ match the people candidate $d$ recently interacted with?''
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Blend the two views with a gate.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{V5.\arabic*},leftmargin=3.6em,start=4]
  \item \textbf{Gate} --- one weight in $(0,1)$ per feature channel:
        \[
          \bm{g} \;=\; \sigma\!\big(W_g\,[\,\bm{h}_{\text{fwd}}\concat\bm{h}_{\text{bwd}}\,]\big)\ \in\ (0,1)^{B\times N\times D}.
        \]

  \item \textbf{Weighted average} of the forward and backward answers:
        \[
          \bm{O} \;=\; \bm{g}\elt\bm{h}_{\text{fwd}} \;+\; (\bm{1}-\bm{g})\elt\bm{h}_{\text{bwd}}.
        \]
\end{enumerate}
Then steps \textbf{C4--C6} run unchanged.

\paragraph{In plain words.}
Ask two questions about the pair (source, candidate): CRAFT's usual one, and its
mirror. Each is a full attention read. The gate then picks, per feature, which
answer to trust more, and averages them. It is the same ``add a second opinion
and gate it'' idea as V4, but the second opinion is a real attention read (not a
pooled vector), and the mix is a weighted average (not an add--on top of CRAFT).

\paragraph{Cost note.}
View 2 builds $B\cdot N$ history sequences of length $L$ (one per candidate)
instead of $B$, so V5 uses much more memory at evaluation time (many
negatives). Lower the batch size if it runs out of memory.

\section{CRAFT\,V8 --- read evidence from outside the history window}
\label{sec:v8}

Code: \code{models/CRAFTV8.py}; the indexes are built by
\code{build\_history\_memory}, \code{build\_covisit\_index} and
\code{get\_edge\_directions} in \code{utils/utils.py}.

\paragraph{The one--sentence idea.}
CRAFT scores a candidate using only an \emph{undirected} window of the source's
last $L$ interactions. V8 keeps all of CRAFT and adds three readouts of evidence
that lies outside that window, then adds their verdict to CRAFT's score.

\paragraph{Why each readout exists (measured on the data).}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item The neighbour sampler symmetrises the graph, so CRAFT cannot tell
        ``$d$ wrote to me'' from ``I wrote to $d$''. On UCI, 60\% of edges answer an
        earlier edge in the opposite direction.
  \item The window truncates long histories: on LastFM, 22.5\% of test edges
        go to an item the user played before, but outside the 120--slot window.
  \item On unseen--dominant bipartite data the source never met the candidate.
        On GoogleLocal, 58.8\% of positives but only 4.9\% of the official
        negatives share a co--visit with the source's history.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{How the evidence is read.}
Every event is indexed once, sorted by (key, time). The number of events of a
key strictly before the prediction time $t$, and the latest of them, are then
two binary searches ($O(\log E)$ per query) on the GPU. No extra neighbours are
sampled. Only events strictly before $t$ are counted. Training queries an index
built from training edges only, and evaluation queries an index built from all
edges, mirroring the train/full neighbour samplers.

\paragraph{Readout R1 --- reciprocity (6 features).}
For each history entry the code recovers whether the source sent or received
it, from the edge id. For each direction $\delta\in\{\text{sent},\text{received}\}$
and each candidate $d$:
\[
  \Big[\ \log\!\big(1+\tfrac{t-t^{\delta}_{\text{last}}(d)}{\kappa}\big),\quad
  \mathbb{1}[\text{exists}],\quad
  \log\!\big(1+n^{\delta}(d)\big)\ \Big],
\]
where $n^{\delta}(d)$ counts window entries with $d$ in direction $\delta$, and
$\kappa$ is a running median of elapsed times (the dataset's time unit).
On bipartite data the ``received'' half is always zero.

\paragraph{Readout R2 --- long--range memory (12 features).}
Over the source's \emph{entire} past, not just the window, with windows
$w_k\in\kappa\cdot\{0.1,1,10,100\}$:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item pair $(s,d)$: $\log(1+\#\text{events in }[t-w_k,t))$ for each $k$,
        $\log(1+\#\text{all time})$, $\mathbb{1}[\text{ever}]$, and
        $\log(1+(t-t_{\text{last}})/\kappa)$;
  \item candidate $d$ with anyone: $\log(1+\#\text{events in }[t-w_k,t))$ for each
        $k$, and the all--time count (popularity momentum).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Readout R3 --- co--visit closure (11 features).}
A co--visit $(i,d)$ is one user interacting with both $i$ and $d$ within $W$
interactions of each other, stamped with the time of the later one. $W=20$,
capped so that the index holds at most $10^8$ co--visits; the cap gives $W\approx6$--$7$
on Yelp, Taobao and ML--20M. For each history item $i_\ell$:
\[
  c_\ell = \#\text{co--visits}(i_\ell,d)\text{ before }t,
  \qquad
  \cos_\ell = \frac{c_\ell}{\sqrt{(1+\mathrm{pop}(i_\ell))(1+\mathrm{pop}(d))}},
\]
giving the sum and maximum of $c_\ell$, the share of history items with
$c_\ell>0$, the sum and maximum of $\cos_\ell$, and the sums of $c_\ell$ and
$\cos_\ell$ weighted by $e^{-(t-t_\ell)/(\kappa\tau_k)}$ for three learned
$\tau_k$. This closes the 4--cycle $s - i_\ell - u - d$: ``people who used what I
used went on to use $d$''.

\paragraph{Combining with CRAFT.}
The $6+12+11=29$ features $\bm{f}_d$ go through a 2--layer MLP, and the result is
added to CRAFT's score behind a gate $g$ initialised to $0$:
\[
  \hat{y}_d \;=\; \underbrace{\mathrm{MLP}_{\text{out}}(\bm{O}_d\concat\bm{\tau}_d\concat\cdots)}_{\text{CRAFT, unchanged}}
  \;+\; g\cdot \mathrm{MLP}_{\text{readout}}(\bm{f}_d).
\]
At initialisation V8 therefore computes exactly CRAFT's score. Each readout can be
ablated (\code{--no\_reciprocity}, \code{--no\_history\_memory},
\code{--no\_covisit}); with all three off, V8 is CRAFT.

\paragraph{In plain words.}
CRAFT asks ``does this candidate fit what the source did lately?''. V8 also asks:
``did the candidate contact the source and wait for an answer?'' (R1), ``has
the source used this candidate before, beyond what the window shows?'' (R2), and
``did other people who used the same things go on to use this candidate?'' (R3).
Each question answers itself with zeros where it has nothing to say, so the
readout MLP learns which question matters on which dataset.

\paragraph{Cost note.}
The training data, loss, negatives and per--dataset hyper--parameters are
CRAFT's. Overhead measured on CPU is about $+3$--$10\%$ per training step. The
indexes are built once per run (seconds on GoogleLocal, minutes on the largest
datasets), and the co--visit index lives on the GPU when it fits under
\code{--covisit\_gpu\_max\_gb}.

\section{Results}

Scores are the test link--prediction metric in percent (MRR$\times 100$ for
GoogleLocal, MOOC, LastFM, YouTube and UCI; the configured validation metric
for Wikipedia). ``$\pm$'' is the sample standard deviation across seeds.
V5 has not been evaluated yet. V8 was evaluated without MRR validation, on
eight datasets (Section~\ref{sec:v8-results}). The full tables, split by which validation
metric was used during model selection --- \emph{with MRR validation} (early
stopping / checkpoint selection driven by validation MRR) versus \emph{without
MRR validation} (a different validation criterion) --- are collected together
in Section~\ref{sec:results-tables} at the end of this note.

\section{Reading of the results}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{V8 is ahead of CRAFT on all eight datasets measured.} The largest
        gains are on the directed, non--bipartite graphs, where reciprocity has
        signal: Flickr $+10.64$, YouTube $+6.60$, UCI $+2.58$. Next come the
        repeat--heavy and unseen--dominant bipartite sets, where long--range
        memory and co--visits apply: Wikipedia $+1.46$, LastFM $+1.27$,
        GoogleLocal $+1.15$. The gain is small on Reddit ($+0.40$) and within
        noise on MOOC ($+0.18$, with V8's seed spread $\pm1.37$). V8 also beats V4
        wherever both were run without MRR validation: GoogleLocal, LastFM,
        YouTube, UCI and MOOC. Two caveats: the CRAFT numbers for Reddit and
        Flickr are single runs, and GoogleLocal V8 has two seeds.
  \item \textbf{V4 wins on both datasets under MRR validation.} Wikipedia $+0.35$, GoogleLocal
        $+0.54$ over CRAFT, and the \emph{smallest} spread across seeds on both.
        The ``add on top of CRAFT'' design (step V4.7) lets the gate fall back to
        plain CRAFT, so the extra path is low--risk. On MOOC (also MRR--validated)
        V4 is ahead on mean ($+0.81$) but with much higher seed variance ($\pm1.83$
        vs.\ $\pm0.88$), driven by one low seed (59.91).
  \item \textbf{V3 helps only on Wikipedia.} $+0.23$ there, but $-1.13$ on
        GoogleLocal, and the noisiest Wikipedia result ($\pm0.21$). The shared
        ``one profile for all candidates'' step (V3.2) assumes a few history
        items dominate for everyone; GoogleLocal's bigger, sparser graph breaks
        that assumption, so forcing every candidate through the same lens throws
        away useful signal.
  \item \textbf{Without MRR validation the picture flips on MOOC.} CRAFT edges
        out V4 there ($61.91$ vs.\ $60.77$), the opposite ranking from the
        MRR--validated MOOC table. On GoogleLocal, LastFM, YouTube and UCI, V4
        stays ahead of CRAFT by small margins ($+0.39$, $+0.12$, $+0.95$, $+0.41$
        respectively) with overlapping or comparable standard deviations, so
        those gaps are modest rather than decisive.
  \item \textbf{What to expect from V5.} It follows V4's winning recipe (second
        signal $+$ gate) but the second signal is a full mirror--direction
        attention read instead of a pooled vector. If the gate behaves it should
        match or beat V4; the risk is that the weighted average (step V5.5) has
        no exact ``fall back to CRAFT'' setting unless the gate $\bm{g}$ goes all
        the way to $\bm{1}$.
\end{itemize}
